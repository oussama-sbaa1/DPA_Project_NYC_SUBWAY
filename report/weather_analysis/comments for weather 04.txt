### Initial findings: Weather and NYC subway ridership

The analysis compares observed subway ridership during different weather conditions with expected ridership estimated from dry-weather observations for the same month, day of the week, and hour.

At the network level, the initial results indicate lower ridership during light rain, heavy rain, and snow. The estimated decreases are 2.0%, 1.0%, and 4.8%, respectively.

The borough-level results show that the relationship varies by location. The Bronx has the largest estimated decrease during heavy rain (10.3%) and snow (9.0%). Snow is associated with lower ridership in all four boroughs. In contrast, Manhattan has a small positive estimate during heavy rain (+3.2%) and almost no change during light rain (−0.3%).

These findings represent associations rather than proof of causation. The baseline uses all available dry-weather observations, and some month/day/hour combinations may have few observations. Other factors, such as holidays, service disruptions, and seasonal travel patterns, may also influence ridership.



### Data quality and analysis exclusions

The Silver tables used in this analysis contain 3,620,146 station-hour ridership records, 445 station-complex reference records, and 8,704 hourly weather records.

Of the weather records, 317 have a missing `condition` value, while 8,387 have a known condition. The analysis excludes the 317 records with missing weather labels because they cannot be reliably categorized as dry, light rain, heavy rain, or snow. These records remain in the underlying weather table; they are excluded from the weather-effect analysis rather than deleted from the source data.

The station-hour and station-complex tables are used to aggregate ridership at the network and borough levels. Additional quality checks are needed to document duplicate records, missing values in other relevant columns, timestamp validity, and identifier consistency. Any records removed or modified by those checks should be counted and the reasons documented.
